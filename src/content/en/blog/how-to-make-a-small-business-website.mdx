You want to make a small business website, but you're not sure where to start: the platform, the pages, the copy, the cost, the timeline. Good news: the technology is the easy part. **What makes a good small business website is what you decide before you open any website builder.**

Launch a simple first version, and track the leads it generates before adding anything else.

## DIY, WordPress or a web design agency?

There's no one-size-fits-all answer. Here's how to choose based on your situation:

| | Website builder | WordPress | Custom site (agency) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best for | Getting started, small budget, time to spare | Flexibility, someone to maintain it | Google visibility and lead generation at the core |
| Who builds it | You | You or a freelancer | The agency, with your content |
| Strengths | Fast, no technical skills needed | Widely used, lots of plugins | Fast loading, built to convert and rank |
| Limits | Less control over SEO and forms | Updates and security to manage | Higher upfront investment |

<Encadre type="astuce">
Whatever you choose, check three things before you sign: the domain is in your name, you can take your content with you if you leave, and lead tracking is set up at launch.
</Encadre>

## Small business website examples: what the good ones share

You don't need to copy a design. Look at the best sites in your trade and your area, and you'll find the same patterns:

- **The headline says what they do and where**, in plain words: "Kitchen remodeling in Austin", not "Transforming spaces".
- **A phone number and a quote button** sit at the top of every page.
- **Real photos** of their own jobs, products or team.
- **Reviews** shown on the homepage, with a link to the Google profile.
- **One page per service**, each with its own photos and FAQs.

## How long does it take to make a small business website?

Timelines depend mostly on you, not on the technology. **Rough, indicative ranges** for a small business:

| Type of site | Typical timeline | What changes it |
|---|---|---|
| Simple DIY site (3 to 5 pages) | A few days to a few weeks | The time you can put in |
| Small business site with a pro (5 to 10 pages) | 3 to 8 weeks | Getting copy and photos ready |
| Custom site with specific features | 2 to 4 months | Number of features and revision rounds |

The classic trap: a site that's technically ready but waits weeks for copy and photos. **Prepare your content while the site is being built**, not after.

## After launch: getting visitors

A new website doesn't show up at the top of Google on its own. To get seen, three levers work together:

- **Your Google Business Profile**, to show up on Maps in your area. Our [Google Business Profile optimization guide](/en/blog/google-business-profile-optimization) covers every setting.
- **Local SEO**, so your service pages rank for searches in your area. See our guide to [what local SEO is and how it works](/en/blog/what-is-local-seo).
- **Ads**, to get leads in the first weeks while your rankings build up. For trades, our guide on [how to get construction leads](/en/blog/construction-leads) shows how the channels fit together.

And if your days are already full, think about what happens after the lead comes in: we explain [small business automation](/en/blog/small-business-automation), starting with quote follow-ups.
