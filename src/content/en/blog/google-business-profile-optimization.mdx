Google spells it out in its own help center: three factors decide the order of local results.

## Optimize every field

| Field | What to do | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|
| **Business name** | Your real business name, nothing else | Adding "Best Plumber Dallas": risk of suspension |
| **Primary category** | The most specific one: "Plumber" rather than "Construction company" | A category that's too broad, or a side business |
| **Secondary categories** | Your other real services ("HVAC contractor") | Adding ten to "cover everything" |
| **Services** | Every service, in your customers' words | Leaving the list empty |
| **Description** | Up to 750 characters: what, for whom, where, why you | Stuffing it with keywords |
| **Photos** | Team, trucks, jobs, added regularly | Three photos from 2019, or stock images |
| **Hours and phone** | Up to date, holidays included, same number as your website | Wrong hours that cost you calls |
| **Updates (posts)** | A finished job, two photos, three lines | A profile untouched for months |

<Encadre type="astuce" titre="Quick win">
Look at the categories used by the competitors ranking at the top in your city (they appear under their name). It's the best clue to what Google expects for your type of business.
</Encadre>

## Reviews: ask, reply, repeat

Reviews matter to Google **and** to customers, who compare ratings before they call. What works best: a text sent right after a successful job, with a direct link to your review form.

  message={'Hi Sarah, thanks again for trusting us with your bathroom remodel. If you’re happy with the work, a quick Google review would mean a lot, it takes 1 minute:\nhttps://g.page/r/…/review\nHave a great day, Mike'}
  reponse="Done! Thanks to the whole crew."

- **Reply to every review**, including negative ones. A calm, factual reply often reassures more than a perfect rating.
- **Consistency beats bursts**: a few reviews every month are worth more than twenty at once and then nothing.

<Encadre type="attention">
Never buy reviews, and never trade them for a discount. It's against Google's rules, and in the US the FTC's rule on fake reviews allows it to fine businesses that buy or sell them.
</Encadre>

## Track your visibility with a GeoGrid

Searching "plumber near me" on your own phone tells you very little: **the result depends on where you're standing**. You can rank #1 outside your office and be invisible two miles away.

A **GeoGrid** lays a grid of points around your business. For each point, a tool simulates a search from a customer at that exact spot and records your position in Google Maps.

## Mistakes that sink a profile

- **Keywords in the business name**: risk of suspension.
- **A fake address** or a virtual office to look closer to a big city: against the rules, and these profiles often end up suspended.
- **Two profiles for the same business**: they compete and can be removed.
- **Inconsistent information** between your profile, website and directories.
- **An abandoned profile**: wrong hours, no recent photos, unanswered reviews.
