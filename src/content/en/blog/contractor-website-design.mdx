A contractor website isn't a brochure. It has one job: **make a homeowner who just found you on Google trust you enough to request a quote**. To do that, it has to show in a few seconds what you do, where, that you're legit, and how to reach you.

<Encadre>
- One page per service, with your service area spelled out.
- Visible proof: real job photos, reviews, license and insurance.
- A short quote form, and a phone number one tap away on mobile.
- Lead tracking, so you know what the site actually brings in.
</Encadre>

## Trust signals that win the job

Letting a contractor into your home takes trust. Your website should answer doubts **before** they come up:

| Trust signal | Where to put it |
|---|---|
| License number and insurance | Footer and contact page |
| Certifications and memberships (trade associations, manufacturer certifications) | Homepage and relevant service pages |
| Customer reviews | Homepage, with a link to your Google Business Profile |
| Job photos | Everywhere, especially on service pages |
| Years in business and team | Homepage or About page |
| Warranty on your work | Service pages and quote form |

<Encadre type="astuce">
In many states, licensed contractors must display their license number in their advertising. Putting it on your website costs nothing, and it's often the detail that makes a homeowner pick you over the next contractor.
</Encadre>

Too long, and people leave. Too short, and you waste time on the phone with out-of-area requests. Here's the difference:

And above all: **respond fast**. A request that waits two days has often gone to a competitor.

## Mistakes that cost you jobs

<Encadre type="attention" titre="Fix these first">
- **A slow or hard-to-read site on mobile.** Most homeowners search on their phone, often in a hurry.
- **No service area written anywhere.** Visitors can't tell if you cover their address, so they leave.
- **Information that doesn't match your Google profile.** A different name, address or phone confuses customers and Google.
- **No tracking.** Without call and form tracking, you can't know whether the site pays off.
</Encadre>

## Measure what your website brings in

A contractor website is judged on one thing: **how many quote requests it generates**. Count form submissions, phone and text clicks, and where they come from (Google, Maps, ads). It's what tells you where to focus next: a service page, your [Google Business Profile](/en/blog/google-business-profile-optimization), or [local SEO](/en/blog/what-is-local-seo) as a whole.
