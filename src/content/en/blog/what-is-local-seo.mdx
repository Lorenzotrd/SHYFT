## Google's 3 local ranking factors

Google explains it in its own help center:

| Factor | What it means | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| **Relevance** | Does your business match the search? | Right category on your profile, one page per service, your customers' words |
| **Distance** | How far are you from the searcher? | Nothing about your address, but pages for the cities you serve |
| **Prominence** | How well known and trusted are you? | Reviews, links from local websites, listings on the right directories |

### Your website: what your profile can't do

Your profile gets you on Maps. **Your website gets you found for many more searches**, and it's what builds trust before the call. For local SEO, it needs:

- **one page per main service**, with real content;
- **one page per main city** if you serve several, with local content (jobs completed there, response times), not a copy where only the city name changes;
- **name, address and phone** in the footer, exactly as on your profile;
- **structured data** (schema markup): invisible code that tells Google, and AI tools, "this is a local business, here's its address, hours and service area".

### Local SEO tips for your reputation

- **Reviews**, requested after every completed job, with a reply to each one.
- **Consistent citations**: an old phone number on a directory or a different address on Facebook muddies the signal. List where you appear and fix it.
- **The right directories**: Yelp, BBB, Nextdoor, Angi, plus your trade association and your city. Not hundreds.
- **Local links**: your chamber of commerce, a team you sponsor, a supplier, local news. These come from real relationships, not from buying them.
