You asked three companies for a website quote: $900, $4,500 and $15,000. Same business, same needs, and a tenfold gap. The cost of a small business website depends less on the technology than on **what the provider actually does for you**: writing, structuring, SEO and tracking.

**In short:** in 2026, expect a few dozen dollars a month for a website you build yourself on a website builder, often $2,000 to $8,000 with a freelancer, and $5,000 to $20,000 or more with an agency for a small business website. On top of that come yearly costs: domain, hosting and maintenance. To compare quotes, look at what each one includes, not just the total.

An **e-commerce website** costs significantly more than a brochure site: product pages, checkout, shipping, inventory. Expect to start around $8,000 with a provider, and well beyond depending on your catalog and integrations.

## What drives the cost of a small business website

| Item | What pushes the price up or down |
|---|---|
| Number of pages | A 5-page site and a 30-page site (one page per service and city) are very different amounts of work |
| Copywriting | You provide the text, or the provider writes it for your customers and for Google |
| Design | Customized template, or fully custom design |
| Features | Quote request form, online booking, client portal, payments, CRM integration |
| SEO | Technical basics only, or keyword research, service area pages and Google Business Profile |
| Photos | Stock images, or real photos of your jobs, team and location |
| Lead tracking | Call and form tracking, reporting dashboard |

The two items that explain most price gaps are **copywriting** and **SEO**. A low quote often assumes you'll write every page yourself, and that nobody will work on your Google visibility.

## Yearly website costs to budget from day one

The build price is only the start. A website has costs every year, and a quote that doesn't mention them hides part of the bill:

| Cost | Rough range |
|---|---|
| Domain name (.com) | About $10 to $25 a year |
| Hosting | A few dollars to a few dozen dollars a month |
| Maintenance (updates, backups, security) | $0 if you do it yourself, $50 to $300 a month with a provider |
| Licenses (theme, plugins, tools) | Varies, often tens to a few hundred dollars a year |
| Changes (new pages, new features) | Billed per request, or included in a plan |

<Encadre type="astuce">
Ask every provider for the **total cost over three years**: the build, plus three years of domain, hosting and maintenance. It's the only fair way to compare an upfront price with a monthly plan.
</Encadre>

## Cheap website or website that pays for itself

Judge a website by what it brings in, not by its price. A simple example: a $5,000 website that brings in three quote requests a month costs, over two years with $50 a month for hosting and maintenance, about $86 per lead ($6,200 for 72 leads). A $900 website that brings in none costs $900 for nothing.

## The bottom line on small business website cost

A small business website can cost anywhere from a few dollars a month to more than $20,000, but the gap almost always comes down to the work included: content, SEO and lead tracking. To choose well, write down your needs, compare quotes line by line, calculate the three-year cost and keep ownership of your site. Then judge it on the only thing that matters: how many leads it brings in.
