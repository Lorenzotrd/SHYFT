Your website is showing its age: it looks off on phones, nobody dares to edit it, and it no longer looks like your business. A small business website redesign makes sense. Just one rule: **don't break what already works**, starting with the pages Google sends you traffic to today.

**In short:** a successful small business website redesign starts with an audit of which pages get visits and leads, and from which searches. Every existing URL is either kept or redirected, the content that ranks is kept and improved, and the launch follows a checklist, with Google Search Console monitored for several weeks.

<Encadre>
- Plan the redesign with your old site's numbers: visited pages, search queries, leads received.
- Every old URL must lead somewhere: the same URL, or a 301 redirect to the matching page.
- Content that ranks gets kept and improved, not deleted to "clean things up".
- Call and form tracking goes live on launch day, not a month later.
</Encadre>

## How to redesign a website without losing SEO

Google has learned to connect your current pages to certain searches. If those pages vanish or move without notice, Google hits dead ends (404 errors) and your rankings can drop. The fix fits in one document: **the redirect plan**.

For every old URL, it lists the new one. A "301" redirect tells Google and visitors: this page has moved permanently, here is its new address.

| Old URL | New URL | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| /our-services.html | /services/bathroom-remodeling | 301 to the closest matching page |
| /remodeling-austin | /remodeling-austin | URL kept, content expanded |
| /news/trade-show-2019 | /projects | 301 to a useful page |
| /contact.php | /contact | 301 |

In the weeks that follow, open Search Console every week: the **Pages** report to spot URLs with errors, the **Performance** report to compare clicks with your audit. A dip for a few days is common; a lasting drop on one specific page usually means a missing redirect or deleted content.

## How much does a website redesign cost?

There's no single price, and be careful comparing quotes that don't describe the same work. The budget comes down to a few line items:

| Line item | What changes the budget |
|---|---|
| Number of pages | An 8-page site and a 60-page site don't need the same content and redirect work |
| Content | Copy and photos you provide, or written and produced by the agency |
| Features | Qualifying quote form, online booking, client portal, CRM connection |
| SEO | Audit, redirect plan, content migration, post-launch monitoring |
| Lead tracking | Call and form tracking, reporting dashboard |

The line item most often missing from proposals is **SEO for the redesign**: the audit, the redirects and the monitoring. Yet it's what protects the leads your site already brings in. Ask every vendor how they handle it.

## Questions to ask before you sign

- **Who owns the domain, the site and the content?** They should be in your name, so you can switch vendors without losing everything.
- **How will the old URLs be handled?** The answer you want: a full inventory and a redirect plan.
- **How will I know what the new site brings in?** Call and form tracking should be planned from the start. Our [conversion tracking](/en/services/data-tracking) page explains what to measure.
- **Who can edit the site afterwards?** You, without calling anyone, for everyday text and photo changes.
